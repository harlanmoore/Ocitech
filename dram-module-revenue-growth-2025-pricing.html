DRAM Module Revenue Rose 59% in 2025. What Does That Mean for RAM Prices?

A new industry report puts the module market at $21.2 billion. The headline signals a strong year for suppliers, but it is not a price index for the memory in your server—or your surplus inventory.

By Ocitech · September 29, 2026 · 4 minute read

THE TAKEAWAYUse industry revenue as market context. Use comparable, part-specific quotes to make purchasing and selling decisions.What the report measuresRevenue versus priceFor buyersFor surplus sellers

Memory market headlines can look like ready-made budget assumptions. A large percentage appears next to “DRAM,” and the temptation is to apply it to the next purchase order or the stock waiting to be sold. That shortcut can produce a misleading valuation.

A September report on the 2025 market

TrendForce’s September 22 release puts global DRAM module revenue at $21.2 billion in 2025, up 59% year over year. It attributes the increase partly to higher prices and concentrated shipments in the second half. The report also describes stronger server orders, constrained chip allocations for other applications and a shift toward DDR5 among module makers. These are historical findings about 2025, published in 2026. Read the original report.

Revenue growth is not the same as price growth

Revenue depends on how many products sell and the prices they achieve. In a market containing multiple products, the mix matters too. A shift toward more expensive items can lift revenue even when the price of a particular item stays unchanged.

Consider a deliberately simplified example. A seller ships 100 identical modules at $100 each, generating $10,000. If it later ships 150 at the same price, revenue reaches $15,000—a 50% increase with no change in the per-module price. This is arithmetic, not an estimate of what happened in the DRAM market.

The same caution applies in reverse: a revenue figure alone cannot tell you how many modules shipped. It cannot establish the resale value of tested pulls, the premium for sealed stock or the price difference between two part numbers.

Keep the market boundary in view as well. TrendForce’s release addresses DRAM module makers. It should not be silently relabeled as a measure of every memory chip, every server-memory transaction or the entire used-hardware market.

For buyers: build the budget from a defined configuration

Use the report to prompt a supplier conversation, then price the requirement you actually have. Record the complete part number, quantity, condition, warranty and delivery date. Ask how long the quote is valid and whether the offered quantity is committed.

If last year’s purchase is the benchmark, check that the comparison is still like for like. Changes in capacity, specification or support terms should be visible before a price movement is described as a market increase.

For a budget covering several possible configurations, obtain separate estimates rather than applying the headline percentage to all of them. Keep the assumptions attached to each estimate so the team can update the right inputs as the project develops.

For surplus sellers: value the lot, not the headline

A strong supplier revenue report is not an appraisal of the modules on your shelf. Start with an inventory that distinguishes complete part numbers and quantities. Add condition and any testing information you can substantiate.

When comparing offers, include inspection terms, shipping responsibilities and payment timing. Two amounts can look comparable while leaving the seller with different costs or obligations. Ask what could change the final settlement before choosing an offer.

Do not use the 59% revenue figure to mark up an old valuation. A current offer for the identified lot is better evidence of its realizable value than a percentage describing a different market and period.

Three labels to keep beside every market statistic

  • Period: When did the activity happen, and when was it reported?
  • Scope: Does the figure describe chips, finished modules, a retail sample or a particular supplier?
  • Measure: Is it revenue, shipment volume, a price index or a forecast?

Those distinctions make industry reporting more useful. They let a manager recognize a meaningful market development without turning it into a claim the data cannot support.

A market statistic can explain the backdrop. A comparable quote tells you what a specific transaction costs.
